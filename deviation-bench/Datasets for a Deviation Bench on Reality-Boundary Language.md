# Datasets for a Deviation Bench on Reality-Boundary Language

## Why this search space is difficult

Your framing is directionally right: if you want to study when language “breaks through reality boundaries,” the closest clinical target is not generic bias or generic mental health support, but **psychosis-spectrum language**, especially phenomena related to **unusual thought content, delusion-like beliefs, perceptual abnormalities, and disorganized speech**. Those are exactly the constructs operationalized in psychosis-risk interviewing frameworks such as SIPS, which explicitly names unusual thought content or delusions, perceptual abnormalities or hallucinations, and disorganized speech as key positive-symptom targets. More broadly, psychiatry and psycholinguistics already treat language in psychosis as a serious signal for formal thought disorder and altered reality testing. citeturn37search14turn27search4turn27search14

That said, **open psychiatric speech and dialogue data are unusually scarce**. A 2022 Schizophrenia Bulletin commentary notes that spoken-language datasets from psychiatric populations are surprisingly hard to obtain, and the current PsychosisBank governance page makes the same point in practice by putting its materials behind membership and controlled-use rules. In contrast, depression and counseling datasets are much easier to access, but most of them capture **distress, support-seeking, or therapeutic style** rather than true “reality-boundary” phenomena. citeturn20search1turn37search2turn1view0turn13view0

There is also a second methodological issue that matters for your benchmark design. Recent work on user miscalibration of AI behavior, observation-sensitive model behavior, and AI-user delusional spiraling all point in the same direction: **context changes behavior**, and multi-turn interaction can matter as much as single-turn task accuracy. That makes a strong case for combining **psychosis data, depression interview data, counseling controls, and non-psychiatric language-disorder controls** in one evaluation stack. citeturn28search3turn28search1turn29search1

## Adjacent clinical control corpora

If your benchmark is meant to detect **context-induced deviation** rather than merely “abnormal language,” you need negative controls from disorders that impair language **without** primarily breaking reality testing. This is where the broader TalkBank clinical ecosystem is unusually useful. TalkBank makes it possible to analyze corpora in a uniform CHAT format, and several clinical banks are available through the same infrastructure, though most are controlled-access. citeturn22search1turn22search5

**DementiaBank** is a shared multimedia database for communication in dementia and mild cognitive impairment, with controlled consortium access; the Pitt Corpus specifically offers browsable and downloadable transcripts and media for the classic Cookie Theft and related tasks. **AphasiaBank** is a shared multimedia database for communication in aphasia, also controlled-access. **FluencyBank** covers stuttering and related fluency disorders; some of its teaching data are open access, while research data are restricted to consortium members. citeturn21search0turn21search2turn21search12turn21search1turn21search3turn22search0turn22search4turn22search7

These corpora matter because they let you ask a stronger scientific question: **is the model reacting to psychosis-like breaks in reality testing, or just to any linguistic irregularity, disfluency, or impairment?** Without this contrast, a benchmark can confuse psychosis, cognitive decline, aphasia, and speech disfluency. citeturn37search14turn21search12turn21search7turn22search0

## A practical acquisition strategy for Deviation Bench

For your specific project, I would build the dataset in **three layers**.

The first layer should be a **core psychosis layer**. If you can get access, prioritize **PsychosisBank DISCOURSE-UWO** first, then **TOPSY**, then **Dauvermann**. DISCOURSE-UWO is the best available match for studying semantic drift, derailment, dream or narrative material, and conversational deviation under multiple elicitation contexts. TOPSY is especially valuable if you want to tie outputs to thought-disorder scales, and Dauvermann gives you a higher-risk but less severe spectrum population. citeturn2view0turn38view1turn38view2

The second layer should be a **real clinical interview control layer**, ideally **PDCH** plus **DAIC-WOZ**. These let you test whether your “deviation” metric is actually picking up psychosis-like reality drift, or whether it mostly responds to depression severity, clinician prompting style, or general psychiatric distress. PDCH is especially attractive because it is real psychiatrist-patient interaction with timestamps, corrected transcripts, turn structure, emotion annotations, and HAMD-17 labels. DAIC-WOZ gives you a widely used English semi-structured benchmark with cleaner acquisition. citeturn13view0turn1view0

The third layer should be a **context and style manipulation layer**, using **AnnoMI**, **HOPE**, **MEMO**, and if needed **Psych8k** or **CounselChat**. These are not strong sources of reality-boundary language, but they are strong sources of **therapeutic framing**, **reflective listening**, **supportive response norms**, and **dialogue-act structure**. That makes them exactly the kind of data you want if the point of Deviation Bench is to ask how much a model’s judgment drifts when the **same underlying semantic content** is wrapped in different interpersonal frames. citeturn10view2turn10view0turn10view1turn24view2turn10view3

If you also want rapid scale in Chinese, add **D4** as a controlled diagnostic-chat layer and **MDD-5k** as a synthetic augmentation layer, but keep those clearly separated from real data in any benchmark reporting. Otherwise you risk collapsing “model drift under clinical context” into “model behavior on generated dialogues.” citeturn35view0turn36search1turn36search7

A defensible minimum viable benchmark would therefore look like this: **PsychosisBank DISCOURSE-UWO + PDCH + DAIC-WOZ + AnnoMI + one TalkBank non-psychiatric control bank** such as DementiaBank or AphasiaBank. That gives you psychosis, depression, semi-structured interviewing, counseling frame, and non-psychotic language impairment in one design. citeturn2view0turn13view0turn1view0turn10view2turn21search0turn21search1

## Open questions and limitations

The biggest limitation is that **truly open psychosis conversation data are still rare**. PsychosisBank is the right direction, but access is mediated through membership and governance rules, and at least one visible corpus page explicitly says the data are not yet public. citeturn37search2turn38view0

A second limitation is that many “mental health dialogue” datasets are **not actually clinical session transcripts**. Some are elicited monologues, some are scraped online Q&A, some are paraphrased question-answer pairs, and some are fully synthetic. That does not make them useless, but it does mean they should not all be treated as the same evidence class. citeturn42view0turn10view3turn24view0turn36search7

A third limitation is that most currently accessible corpora do **not** provide utterance-level labels for delusions, hallucinations, or reality-testing failures. They often provide global diagnoses, severity scales, or thought/language indices instead. So if your benchmark needs a direct measure of “boundary crossing,” you will probably need to add your own annotation layer on top of the raw transcripts. citeturn38view1turn38view2turn13view0

The bottom line is that the best available path is not one dataset but a **stack**: **PsychosisBank for psychosis-specific language**, **PDCH and DAIC-WOZ for real interview controls**, **AnnoMI/HOPE/MEMO for counseling context controls**, and at least one **non-psychiatric language-disorder bank** to prevent your metric from conflating psychosis with general disorder-related language disruption. citeturn2view0turn13view0turn1view0turn10view2turn10view0turn10view1turn21search0turn21search1
